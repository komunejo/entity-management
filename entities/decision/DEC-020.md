Implements [raw-text scan: pre-parse checks for what the parse destroys](../proposal/PROP-006.md)^[PROP-006](../proposal/PROP-006.md), resolving [the unquoted comma is one of a class — silent misparses the parse itself hides](../issue/ISSUE-003.md)^[ISSUE-003](../issue/ISSUE-003.md). The design decisions the implementation settled, where the proposal left the implementer a choice or said nothing:

**Duplicate keys are caught by a stricter loader, not a scan rule.** The proposal fixed the policy (never admissible) and left the mechanism open. A `SafeLoader` subclass that errors on a repeated key wins because it sees every mapping at every depth — a raw-text rule would have needed indentation tracking to know which lines share a mapping, which is parser work, and the scan is deliberately not a parser. Consequence: duplicate-key errors surface through the "not valid YAML" path with a line number, not as scan findings.

**The frontmatter-truncation member is fixed in the splitter, not detected by the scan.** `split_frontmatter` now accepts a closing delimiter at column zero only. An indented `---` is never a legal close and always a legal line of a multi-line value, so no ambiguity exists and nothing needs flagging — the cut simply stops happening. This is the one member of ISSUE-003's class whose fix removes the hazard instead of naming it.

**The flow-collection rule flags multi-word unquoted scalars, not scalars "containing structural characters".** The proposal's phrasing described the author's intent; on raw text it is unimplementable as written, because a structural character *terminates* an unquoted scalar — by the time the scan sees the text, the scalar it wants to inspect has already ended at the comma that was the problem. What is detectable is the precondition: prose standing unquoted where structure characters are live. Multi-word is the tell — single-word scalars (tags, enum values, IDs) cannot lose a tail they do not have, and stay silent.

**The scan's jurisdiction is every YAML the engine reads** — record frontmatter, schema files, and `entity-manager.yaml` — not the Markdown bodies ([raw-text scan for the Markdown side: recorded, and declined](../proposal/PROP-007.md)^[PROP-007](../proposal/PROP-007.md)).

**The engine's own emissions meet the scan's rules**: `new` double-quotes the title it interpolates. An engine that plants what its validator hunts would fail its own contract the moment a title carried a `#`.

**Flag handling is a documented protocol, not folklore**: the triage discipline (claim your own slips, resolve with the session's criterion, escalate at most one intent question, record the criteria the human's answers establish) lands as `references/troubleshooting.md` in the skill, with a human-facing companion (`docs/troubleshooting.md`, repository documentation) explaining every engine message with examples. Session-start validation joins the firing points in `references/hooks.md` so that flag ownership is decidable by construction: from a green start, every later flag belongs to the session's own work.

Verified before release: the four measured members reproduce and are caught (flow comma, ` #` truncation, duplicate keys at every depth, indented `---`); twenty legitimate constructs — single-word flow, block prose with commas, comments after empty values, block scalars holding hostile text, nested combinations of all of these — stay silent; this repository's 42 records validate green with zero scan findings.
